Lab Troubleshooting Insights

Real Hardware Lessons Learned

1. Why Ultrasonic Replaced RFID

When the RFID module wouldn't communicate reliably with the pin stack, we engineered an ultrasonic proximity trigger (`< 2cm`) paired with a piezo buzzer (Pin 38) to replicate real-world contactless tap-to-pay registers.

2. Floating Joystick Pin Runaway Prevention

During testing, we discovered that if a joystick jumper wire disconnects while powered, floating analog signals cause the motor driver to spin a stepper clockwise uncontrollably. Defining an explicit neutral deadband (`else don't move`) eliminates jitter and runaway motion.

3. 3-Motor Simultaneous CNC Shield Control

Most tutorials only drive 1 or 2 steppers at once. We mapped discrete directional states (`+1`, `-1`, `Left`, `Right`) from the 2-potentiometer gimbal joystick to coordinate all 3 lead-screw steppers smoothly.

Failure Analysis, Root-Cause Diagnosis & Redo Iteration

Engineering Post-Mortem & Hardware Debugging Log

How I diagnosed a thermal polyfuse trip on the CNC shield, bypassed shorted GPIO pins, and solved continuous-rotation servo runaway loops

ISSUE 01 // POWER RAILS

Power Distribution & Short-Circuit Rail Isolation

Bridging power across breadboard rails—a wiring layout that worked in Tinkercad simulation—caused an accidental real-world short circuit that damaged several digital GPIO pins and heated the CNC shield's onboard thermal polyfuse under stepper driver current draw.

Fix: Isolated the servo load onto a dedicated breadboard power supply module to prevent logic brownouts.
ISSUE 02 // GPIO RECOVERY

Single-Joystick State Multiplexing

Because the breadboard short damaged several microcontroller pins mid-deadline, running two separate joysticks was no longer viable on the affected headers.

Fix: Consolidated control onto a single working analog joystick and used the pushbutton to switch states between controlling the X/Y/Z stepper gantry and driving the servo motors.
ISSUE 03 // SERVO WINCH PWM

Counterweighted Winch & ±1 Incremental Control

An initial SG90 micro servo lacked the mass to counterbalance the claw without being held by hand. Switching to a heavier continuous-rotation servo winch solved the physical balance, but standard positional commands (`90` or `180`) locked the winch in continuous rotation.

Fix: Drove the servo winch like a stepper—incrementing or decrementing by ±1 degree per joystick read—to achieve smooth, non-blocking vertical spooling.
